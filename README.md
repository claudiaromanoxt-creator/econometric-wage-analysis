1. Quantitative Data Analysis (Python)

Project: Estimating the Union Wage Premium

File: Assignment_lab_wage premium.ipynb

An empirical data analysis project estimating the union wage premium for a sample of young male workers in the 1980s.  
IPYNB

Data Preparation: Cleaned and segmented panel data using pandas and numpy.  
    

Exploratory Data Analysis: Created visualizations (matplotlib) to explore wage gaps across union status, educational attainment, and racial groups.  
    

Econometric Modeling: Estimated the causal effect of union membership on wages. The Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression was implemented both manually via linear algebra and using statsmodels to add demographic controls.  
    

Bootstrapping: Wrote a custom bootstrap resampling script to calculate empirical standard errors and 95% confidence intervals for the wage premium.  

2. Policy Research & Literature Reviews

Paper 1: Psychological Barriers to Labor Market Participation: Evidence from Rural Ghana

File: Psychological barriers to participation in the labor market_policy brief.pdf

An analytical review of the Randomized Controlled Trial (RCT) presented in "Psychological barriers to participation in the labor market: Evidence from rural Ghana" by Carvalho et al. (2026).  

Analysis: Examines linear probability models to show that mental health acts as a barrier to traditional job take-up, but this effect dissipates when work-from-home options are introduced.  

Policy Takeaway: Argues that employment programs in developing contexts should integrate mental health frameworks and prioritize human well-being, rather than focusing solely on productivity and efficiency metrics.  
    
Paper 2: Unequal Benefits of AI in the Labor Market: A Gender Perspective

File: Unequal benefits of AI in the labor market_A gender perspective.pdf

A literature review analyzing the macroeconomic impact of Artificial Intelligence on labor market polarization, based on the findings of "Artificial Intelligence and jobs: Evidence from online vacancies" (Acemoglu et al., 2022) and "The occupational impact of Artificial Intelligence: Labor, skills, and polarization" (Felten et al., 2019).  

Analysis: Cross-references the econometric displacement and wage premium coefficients from the aforementioned studies with OECD demographic data to highlight how AI-driven automation disproportionately impacts female-dominated sectors.  
    Policy Takeaway: Discusses the regulatory context of the EU AI Act, emphasizing the need for gender-sensitive indicators and oversight to prevent algorithmic bias and mitigate the widening gender gap in STEM.
